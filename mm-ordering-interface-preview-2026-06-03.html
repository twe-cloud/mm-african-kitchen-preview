01 / Customer order

Ordering feels like the website, not a third-party portal.

Menu cards, modifiers, pickup/delivery choice, tips, and hosted payment stay in M&M's branded path before any POS or delivery vendor sees the order.

  • Customer relationship starts on the M&M website.
  • Modifiers preserve kitchen instructions.
  • Payment confirmation becomes the source of truth.

02 / Staff board

Accept once, then move the order through the room.

The staff board is the bridge between the website and the kitchen. It can operate without TouchBistro while still leaving room for POS adapters later.

  • New paid orders land in a visible queue.
  • Staff accepts, fires to kitchen, marks ready, then completes.
  • Printer and delivery actions stay on the same order card.

03 / Kitchen display

The kitchen sees phases, modifiers, and urgency.

This is the simple KDS path for a tablet or monitor. It does not need to look like a corporate POS. It needs to be readable during service.

  • Accepted, in-kitchen, and ready lanes stay separated.
  • Modifiers are visible on the ticket.
  • Staff can bump tickets without touching the customer site.

04 / Printed ticket

Fallback hardware keeps the kitchen from waiting on POS access.

A simple print ticket is not glamorous, but it is resilient. It gives the kitchen a physical backup while the tablet board and future integrations mature.

  • Thermal-printer friendly ticket format.
  • Order ID, customer, items, modifiers, and subtotal.
  • Works as a low-cost bridge while POS decisions are open.

05 / Uber Direct dispatch

Delivery becomes a rail, not the owner of the order.

The website owns the order and payment first. Uber Direct is used only when a delivery quote is approved and the kitchen is ready to hand off.

  • M&M keeps the customer and order record.
  • Delivery fee and support policy must be approved before launch.
  • Courier dispatch can stay separate from POS dependency.
